Spruce Grove

A local-first coding-agent workbench, built in the open. Prepared for Bill, Zak, and their board guest — shown live in four minutes, not told in twenty.

→ arrow keys to walk

I · the problem

Agents got smart.
The desk around them didn't.

Agent work happens in browser tabs — away from the repo, away from the record, away from the machine that owns the work. The context lives in one window and the work in another, and nothing about either is built to be watched, steered, or trusted.

II · what it is

A thin shell around a proven engine

the engine

The spruce-grove CLI — a full agent harness covered by 363 test files, gated on every push. Tools, hooks, plugins, sessions.

the shell

A Tauri 2 webview that owns no agent logic. Every prompt spawns the CLI in your working directory and streams back as it lands.

the bet

Engines improve weekly; a shell should not churn. Keep the shell thin and the engine swappable — the architecture makes that a rule, not a hope.

III · what it does

Four surfaces, one working animal

speak or type

Live streaming sessions — the agent's thinking, its tool calls with status chips, and per-turn token counts, all as they happen.

dictate on-device

Mic → local Whisper → an editable prompt. The audio never leaves the machine. Speak the task, review the words, then send.

look in live

A browser panel shows screenshots and the action log while the agent works. Seeing is the trust model.

inspect everything

One keypress: branch, commits, dirty tree, pull requests, CI runs — read live from git and gh, not from a cached dashboard.

IV · why local

zero telemetry · zero added network calls

The work stays on your machine

The shell adds no network calls of its own — the engine's own provider traffic is the only wire. Personas bind the agent to allowed directories and feature grants, enforced at the action choke points. Repo-level access is GitHub's own viewerPermission — no invented roles, no parallel permission system.

V · the proof

Tested at the seams most projects forget

the contract

A recorded agent session is replayed through the real protocol router on every test run. Protocol drift fails loudly, first.

the chassis

16 Rust unit tests cover the parser seams — streaming, cancellation, session resume, inspector feeds, settings validation.

the glass

A Chromium suite drives the real UI JavaScript against a mocked bridge; CI builds the bundle on macOS, Windows, and Ubuntu.

The steer.Redirect the agent mid-run. It changes course without losing the session.

VII · quality, in the open

Even the QA was a swarm

Five AI agents tested this build end-to-end this week — cold start, the conversation loop, every panel, accessibility and layout, and the deck you are watching. They found one real bug: a crash on the Escape key, caught, fixed, and regression-tested the same day. Every other finding is filed in a public tracker. Nothing is hidden, including the flaws.

And the belief underneath: the people closest to the problem, given a voice and a tool that keeps itself honest, will out-build any monolith that schedules their imagination for the next fiscal year.

X · why it works

The mechanics, plainly

Thin shell, strong engine.The desktop owns no agent logic; the tested engine is swappable by design.
The contract is pinned.A recorded session replays through the real protocol router on every test run — drift fails loudly, first.
Local-first.The work stays on your machine. Zero telemetry, zero added network calls — a stance, not a slogan.
Trust through seeing.Streaming thoughts, tool cards, live look-in, the inspector — nothing happens that you cannot watch.
It tends itself.The grove checks its own pulse, heals, reports every self-repair in plain words — and consent survives autonomy.

XI · the pledge

the machine stays free · the value flows to the misfits

Where every dollar goes

Any profit the grove's work ever makes is ledgered in public and donated to the 43 Institute (43i.org) — Ken Block's foundation: a 501(c)(3) creating pathways for underserved, highly driven "motivated misfits" in action sports, motorsports, and the creative arts.

why 43i

Kids who love things loudly and lack the means to chase them — racing and rally, skateboards and BMX, art and music and print. Mentorship pathways, access, skills, health. Their own story: a kid in a screen-printing class whose art project became DC Shoes.

we recognize the species

The grove's own proof is a motivated misfit handed a pathway — no college degree, a director of the work at thirty-five. The Method is a mentorship pathway. Talent is evenly distributed; means are not.

the transparency

Donations to 43i are unrestricted and used where needed most — their words. Our ledger of earned and given is published beside the work. No quiet skimming, ever. Their impact, in their words: 43i.org/impact

XII · the root

The Leather Apron Club, 1727

Benjamin Franklin's Junto — the Leather Apron Club — was a mutual-improvement circle that turned curiosity into civic infrastructure. Its operating rules are this grove's constitution:

Love truth for truth's sake.Zero invented facts. Verify, or say plainly: not verified.
Mutual aid."How can the Junto assist you in any of your honorable designs?" — every session starts from your goal, not the tool's features.
Observe, inquire, prototype.Read, understand, make the smallest change that works — in code, and in the room.
Every learner becomes a trainer.The index card travels. This is how a tool reaches seventy-five thousand.

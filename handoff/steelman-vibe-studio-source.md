## What he is launching

**Vibe Studio** — a build-space for University of Arkansas students.

Tyler's account of the shape:

- **Access from day one.** Students get into the tools immediately, not after
  a credential, a course sequence, or a budget cycle.
- **Across every major.** Explicitly not CS-only — the pull is students from
  business, design, agriculture, health, and everywhere else, building things
  in their own domain.
- **Micro-grants + hardware loans.** Some access is funded through small
  grants and equipment lending, so *cost is not the gate*. *(Confirm the exact
  mechanism and source — this is the detail most likely to be paraphrased.)*

## Why it matters to us (the honest read)

This is the **same instinct** the deck already argues:

| This deck says | Vibe Studio does |
|---|---|
| "every learner a trainer" — observe, inquire, prototype, hand the card on | gives students a first build, then a reason to teach the next one |
| "meet people where they are" | day-one access instead of gatekeeping by prerequisite |
| the Junto — mutual improvement becoming civic infrastructure | a campus room where that loop can actually run at scale |

**The gap it exposes:** a campus full of *first builds* needs a harness that
keeps the work **local, auditable, and theirs**. That is precisely what
Spruce Grove is. Students building across all majors will otherwise rent a
closed cloud tool by default and learn a dependency before they learn a craft.

**So the ask-shaped sentence is:** *Vibe Studio is the distribution; Spruce
Grove is the sovereign harness underneath it.* Not "adopt our tool" — "here is
the local-first floor a program like this can stand on."
